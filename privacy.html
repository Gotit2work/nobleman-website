Who we are

Nobleman Productions is a video production company in San Diego and Las Vegas, founded by Jean Gotay and Justin Edelman. GotIT2Work runs this website and the client portal for them. In this notice, “we” means both.

For any question about this notice or your information, email alexis@gotit2work.com.

02

What you send us

When you use the Start a project form, we receive what you type: your name and email address and, if you add them, your phone number, company, the service and budget range you pick, dates, city or venue, and project details.

If you email us directly, we receive your message and your email address.

We use this only to reply to you, to plan and quote your project, and to keep a record of our conversation.

03

What your browser sends automatically

Every time your browser asks any website for a page, it sends some technical details with the request. These include your IP address (a number that identifies your internet connection and roughly where it is), your browser and device type, the page you asked for, and the time.

Our host, Vercel, records these in server logs. We use them only to keep the sites secure and working. We don’t use them to identify you or to build a profile of you.

04

This website sets no cookies of its own, and it runs no advertising or analytics trackers.

The client portal sets one cookie, and only when you sign in. It keeps nothing else in your browser.

Films

Films on this website play through Vimeo or YouTube, including the home page’s background video, which plays from Vimeo. We turn on Vimeo’s do-not-track setting, which tells its player not to set cookies or collect viewing statistics. YouTube films use YouTube’s privacy-enhanced mode, which doesn’t store information about you until you press play. Once a YouTube film plays, Google may use its own cookies under its own policy.

Films in the portal play from wherever we keep that project’s videos: Vimeo or YouTube as above; Frame.io or Wistia, whose files play in the portal’s own player straight from their storage; or, for a video we’ve linked, Google Drive, Loom, or Dropbox, whose own players load under their own policies.

05

The client portal

The demo keeps nothing. Anyone can open the portal’s demo and click through a sample project without signing in. Notes, approvals, and messages you try there aren’t saved or sent to anyone.

There’s no public sign-up: we create an account for each person who needs one. For each account the portal keeps:

  • your name, email address, and job title, if you give one;
  • your password, stored only in scrambled form (a “hash”) that can’t be turned back into the password, so nobody, including us, can read it;
  • your role (for example decision maker or reviewer), which sets what you can do;
  • when you last signed in, when you last used the portal (so we don’t email you about things while you’re already looking at them), and whether you’ve chosen your own password yet;
  • whether you want email updates;
  • if you turn on two-step sign-in: its key, encrypted, and your recovery codes, stored only as hashes.

Your company’s decision makers can add colleagues to the portal. They give us the colleague’s name, email address, and role, and the colleague gets an email to set up their account.

What you do in the portal is kept with your company’s project, with your name and the time:

  • notes you leave on a version, and the moment in the film each one points to;
  • versions you approve or ask to change, and what you asked for;
  • messages, and which ones you’ve read;
  • files you send us: the file itself in private storage, plus its name, size, and type;
  • videos you send us, which go straight to our Vimeo account, where only we can see them;
  • share links you create to a finished film, who created each one, when it ends, and how many times it has been opened. We don’t record who opens a share link;
  • milestones you confirm, such as a filming day.

Activity log. So we know a delivery landed and can help if something goes wrong, the portal records what happens in it, with the person’s name, the time, and the IP address: signing in, watching a version or film (once a day per film), downloading, approving, creating or turning off share links, changes to your team, and changes our staff make. Only our staff can see it. Entries are deleted after about 13 months.

Emails. The portal emails you invitations, sign-in links, and password reset links when you or we ask for them (each link works once and expires; we keep only a scrambled copy, deleted a week after it’s used or expires), a receipt when you approve a version, and a reminder before a review date we’ve set. If you leave updates on, we also email you when we share a new version, film, or file, or message you. Our team gets an email when you leave a note, approve or ask for changes, message us, send something, confirm a milestone, or change your team, and it includes what you wrote. You can turn your updates off under Account in the portal.

To stop people guessing passwords and codes, the portal also keeps a short record of failed sign-ins, two-step codes, and requests for emailed links: the email address that was tried and the IP address it came from. These records are deleted after about a day.

Project tracking. We may keep a copy of each project’s status in Notion, our planning tool: the project and company name, stage, progress, whether a version is waiting or approved, how many notes are open, the next milestone, and review dates. It doesn’t include your name, email, notes, or messages.

The films you watch and download in the portal are stored with the video service we use for that project (section 06).

07

How long we keep it

  • Inquiries and emails: in our business email for as long as we need them to work with you, plus any records the law requires us to keep, such as invoices. Ask, and we’ll delete the rest.
  • Server logs: Vercel keeps them for a limited time under its own policy. We don’t copy or store them anywhere else.
  • Portal accounts: until the account is closed. Closing an account deletes it and stops its sign-in at once.
  • Notes, decisions, messages, and files stay with the project, under the name of the person who added them, even after that person’s account is closed. Deleting a project deletes them all, including its stored files. Ask, and we’ll delete yours sooner.
  • Videos sent through the portal stay in our Vimeo account until we delete them there.
  • Activity log: about 13 months.
  • Emailed links: a week after they’re used or expire.
  • Share links: with the project, until it’s deleted. A link that’s turned off or has ended stops working at once.
  • Failed sign-in records: about a day.
08

What we don’t do

  • We don’t sell or rent your information, or share it for advertising.
  • We don’t use advertising or analytics trackers, and we don’t follow you across other websites.
  • We don’t add you to a mailing list.

Because we don’t track you across sites, a browser’s Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal doesn’t change how our sites behave. You already get that treatment.

Our sites are for businesses and adults. They aren’t meant for children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect information from them. If you think a child has sent us information, email us and we’ll delete it.

11

Keeping it safe

  • Both sites only load over an encrypted connection (HTTPS).
  • Portal passwords are stored only as a hash, never as the password itself, and nobody sets a password for you: you choose it from a one-time link.
  • You can turn on two-step sign-in, so a code from your phone is needed as well as your password.
  • The portal’s sign-in cookie can’t be read by scripts on the page, and sign-in pauses after repeated failed attempts.
  • Keys the portal uses to reach video services and Notion are stored encrypted.
  • Each client company only sees its own projects. Files are kept in private storage, and each download link stops working after ten minutes.
  • Changing or resetting a password, or a change of role or email, signs that account out everywhere.

No system is perfectly secure. If something ever went wrong that affected your information, we would tell you as the law requires.
